Your coach's priorities · shared October 22

Your coach says you have a great shot. He wants you to use it more in games, be more physical, and scan for threats and open teammates. Ready feet and a quick release help you turn that shot into more attempts.

This is now the direction for your plan. The earlier film examples support these habits; they do not replace the coach's view across several games. His puck-watching observation is coach feedback, not a new finding from our clips.

What to do on your next shift

  1. Compete for the puck and your space. Move your feet to engage in the battle, establish position and work to win or recover possession. Use the physical technique your coach teaches. Measure involvement and possession outcomes, not the number or size of hits.
  2. Be ready to put pucks toward the net. Get your skates up ice, present your stick and prepare before the pass arrives. When there is a usable lane, take the attempt promptly. If the lane is closed, make a small adjustment or move it and get available again. Do not let searching for a perfect shot turn into passing up useful attempts.
  3. Keep your head on a swivel. Before receiving or retrieving, locate pressure and an option. Away from the puck, check the middle/back-side threat and recheck when the puck changes sides. Find an open teammate as well as an opponent.

The coach did not set a numerical shot target or prescribe specific drills. The activities and tracking below are proposed ways to practice his priorities. Physical work belongs in coach-led practice, using the rules and technique taught for Daniel's team.
